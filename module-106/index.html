What Module 106 covers

Two units in one session, and one idea joining them: everything that evaporates or fails to liquefy comes back into the process instead of going to the flare. Unit 360 compresses the flash gas and the boil-off and finds it a route; Unit 370 heats what leaves the cold boxes until it is fuel gas the engines will accept.

  • Follow the gas in flow order — where boil-off comes from, why it never stops, and the three routes out of the compressors, fuel gas first.
  • Open the compressor package. The oil-flooded screw again — third module running, same machine and the same maintenance schedule, which is the point of teaching it again.
  • Read the recycle route as a cleanliness problem, not a routing one: that gas goes to the molecular sieves, which is why PK-362 carries an extra cleaning train.
  • Take it through starting and stopping — the permissives, the sequences, and what trips a package and brings up the other one.
  • Then a unit with almost no handles. In Unit 370 the flows are set upstream; the only lever on the skid is hot oil, and operator control is two temperature loops.

What boil-off and flash gas are, and why they never stop

Three origins, three different clocks
● BOIL-OFF — always
  • TA-221: atmospheric, single containment
  • API 620 / EN 14620 · perlite insulated
  • heat crosses it every hour of every day
  • does not care if the plant is running
● FLASH GAS — while LNG is made
  • liquid comes from liquefaction, Unit 350
  • let down across LV-2211 into storage
  • boils in the valve, caught in V-221
  • variable — it follows the LNG rate
● VAPOUR RETURN — while a truck loads
  • cold vapour back from truck loading
  • liquid in means vapour out of the tanker
  • intermittent: it comes with the bay
  • the manual counts it with the boil-off
And if nobody takes the gas away
1

heat keeps crossing the perlite — liquid keeps boiling

2

the tank is atmospheric: it cannot absorb the pressure

3

PIC-2202 opens TSV-2202 — controlled venting

4

the plant releases gas it already paid to liquefy

  • Unit 360 is what stands between heat ingress and that vent. The manual gives the unit two duties in one line: continuous handling of the boil-off generated by heat ingress and loading operations, and controlled pressure stabilisation of LNG storage and transfer — recovered as fuel gas or recycled upstream of dehydration, in order to minimise flaring (§9.1, §9.4).
  • The tank sets the pace, because it is the only arrival that is always there. Flash follows the liquefaction rate and the vapour return follows the trucks. What the machines are asked to hold is therefore a suction pressure, on a header whose one guaranteed feed is the slowest of the three.
  • FV means full vacuum, and it is on the datasheet because it can happen. Machines can pull down a header fed by a slowly boiling tank — which is why CV-3601 is provided for start-up recirculation, compressor suction protection and stable running at low load (§9.4).

What keeps it inside the pipe
  • You cannot smell this gas. The detectors are your sense of smell, which is why a bagged or bypassed detector is a plant-stopping defect, not a work order for Monday.
  • The seal is the barrier. External inspection and seal check are daily, together with the oil level in the separator and in the mechanical-seal drain pot; the seal leak rate is measured and assessed every 1.000 running hours.
  • Oil tells you first. Quality check every 1.000 h or 6 months, whichever comes first; renewal at 12 months, and earlier on condition if it darkens and the analysis confirms it. 21044-MN-PRO-00001 Rev.00 §6.7
The manual says it plainly — §9.7. This unit handles flammable natural gas under pressure and loss of containment may result in fire, explosion or asphyxiation; the packages add rotating equipment, pressurised oil and hot surfaces. On gas leakage, abnormal vibration, fire or emergency-shutdown activation: follow the plant emergency procedures and evacuate the area if required.
The heater is live when the machine is not. X-361 and X-362 are energised only while their compressor is shut down, to hold the oil warm and stop gas dissolving into it: 2 × 1,725 kW of elements on the datasheet (PR31, PR33) and 3,7 kW of connected load at 690 Vac in the electrical register — the difference is the 0,25 kW element motor. A stopped package is still a hot surface and a live 690 V circuit.
