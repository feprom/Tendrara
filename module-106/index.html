What Module 106 covers

Two units in one session, and one idea joining them: everything that evaporates or fails to liquefy comes back into the process instead of going to the flare. Unit 360 compresses the flash gas and the boil-off and finds it a route; Unit 370 heats what leaves the cold boxes until it is fuel gas the engines will accept.

  • Follow the gas in flow order — where boil-off comes from, why it never stops, and the three routes out of the compressors, fuel gas first.
  • Open the compressor package. The oil-flooded screw again — third module running, same machine and the same maintenance schedule, which is the point of teaching it again.
  • Read the recycle route as a cleanliness problem, not a routing one: that gas goes to the molecular sieves, which is why PK-362 carries an extra cleaning train.
  • Take it through starting and stopping — the permissives, the sequences, and what trips a package and brings up the other one.
  • Then a unit with almost no handles. In Unit 370 the flows are set upstream; the only lever on the skid is hot oil, and operator control is two temperature loops.

what this area does — 21044-PR-MAN-00001 §9.1 and §9.4
it exists so the plant does not flare

An LNG tank boils, a tanker pushes vapour back, the separator flashes — and none of it stops. Area 360 handles all the flash gas and boil-off generated downstream of liquefaction and storage and recovers it as fuel gas, or recycles it upstream of dehydration, to minimise flaring and stabilise the plant. Without it, that gas goes to the cold flare.

the superheater is what makes it possible

These are oil-flooded screw machines and they cannot be fed liquid. The manual sets a design minimum of −60 °C at suction, and the heat that gets the gas there is the machines own discharge, in the tubes of the same shell — no hot oil, no element. Then 6 to 11 barg out of PK-361.

and fuel gas is served first

Under all operating scenarios fuel gas has priority. Only when that demand is met and the header pressure rises does PK-362 start and reinject the gas upstream of Unit 310, as intermittent regeneration gas for the dryers. CV-3601 is neither of those: it is start-up and low-load stability.

How an oil-flooded screw compressor works

  • Two meshing helical rotors in a close-clearance cast-iron casing · 10 barg, −60 / 120 °C. Gas enters between the lobes at the suction end.
  • The pocket seals, then shrinks. It is cut off from suction and squeezed as it travels axially until it uncovers the discharge port. Vi 3,6 is how much it shrinks first — 1997 m³/h swept, at 2950 rpm.
  • Oil is injected into the compression space, and the manual gives it four jobs, not three: it lubricates the bearings, seals between the rotors, removes the heat of compression — and it is the hydraulic fluid of the slide valve. No oil pressure, no capacity control and no start.
  • So the discharge is an oil/gas mixture, not gas — 6"-NG-361109 (C1A) and 6"-NG-361209 (C1B) into 6"-NG-361301, to PK-361-V1. The separator is the second half of the compressor and the oil reservoir of the package. That is Block 4.
  • Positive displacement — no surge, no stall. Close the outlet and it keeps pumping until something fails. So each machine carries its own relief: PSV-3611A / PSV-3611B, blocked outlet case, set 10 barg on PR30 — the same number as the casing design pressure.
  • Therefore capacity cannot be throttled with a valve. It is changed by the slide valve, 100 % → 15 % — next slide.

Capacity control — the slide valve

  • What it is: a block moving lengthwise along the rotors, changing the effective compression length. Towards discharge → more of the trapped gas is actually compressed, capacity rises; towards suction → a port opens and part of it is returned to suction before compression completes — nothing is throttled (§9.7.1).
  • The range: 100 % down to 15 % of rated capacity, continuous, no step changes — manual §9.4, not the vendor capacity table. Below it, §9.4 gives hot-gas bypass as what holds the machine steady. The 25 % of PK-351 does not travel to Unit 360.
  • How it moves: hydraulically, on the machine's own lubrication oil. No separate hydraulic pack on the skid — solenoids send oil to one side of the actuator or the other · Danfoss P/N 018F4704, Table 9.6. Low oil pressure unloads and stops the machine, per the Cause & Effect Matrix.
  • Who commands it: the PLC, from suction pressure PT-3611A/B (PK-361-C1A/B inlet, PCS AI) — the machine holds its own suction, and the capacity is whatever that costs. At start-up it is held fully unloaded, below 5 %, then loaded progressively.
  • Solenoids — and the actuation is not the same on the two packages. 4 per compressor on PK-361 (ZEV-3611 / 3612 / 3613 / 3614 A and B) against 2 per compressor on PK-362 (ZEV-3622 / 3624 A and B): 12 in the unit, all PCS digital outputs, all one service in the register. The connection diagram energises them in pairs, one pair per direction of travel — never one on its own.
  • The feedback is analogue, and identical on both packages: one position transmitter per compressor, ZT-3611A/B and ZT-3621A/B, PCS AI — not a limit switch. The system knows where the slide is, so demand against ZT is a diagnosis. That is also why the 1 000 h check is on slide translation SPEED: a slow slide is an oil fault before it is a control fault.

Torque crosses. Oil does not.
  • Outer rotor on the motor shaft, inner rotor on the pump shaft, a containment shell between them. Torque is transmitted magnetically across it and the two halves stay completely separated: no dynamic seals, leak-free operation. §9.7.6
  • The magnets have a maximum torque. Overload it and the coupling decouples — the pump rotates without effective torque transmission → loss of oil circulation, which must be detected promptly. The motor keeps turning and keeps drawing current. Dry running shall be strictly avoided. §9.7.6
  • So you watch pressure, never the motor.
