Operation · filtration

How the filtration works

Filter cutaway — body, multistage element, auto drain
Filter housings
Coalescing element
Carbon element
  • Particulate filters have differential-pressure indicators, auto drains & multistage elements — 2 grades in series to extend element life.
  • Activated-carbon filters have no ΔP indicator and no drain — the element absorbs oil from the compressed air.
  • Air path through the housing: air inlet → filter media (multistage) → liquids — water & compressor oil — coalesce on the element and drip to the bottom → auto drain (particulate only).
  • Carbon filters are installed after the refrigerated dryer: lower air temperature = longer element life.
Operation · refrigerated dryer — PK-502

How the refrigerated dryer works

Refrigerated dryer circuit — click to enlarge
  • Compressed air is cooled to +3 to +5 °C; the moisture condenses, making the air «dry» at normal ambient temperature.
  • The pressure dew point becomes +5 °C — no moisture will condense unless the air drops below 5 °C.
  • An air-to-air heat exchanger pre-cools the incoming air for efficiency & re-heats the discharge, so condensation does not form on cold pipes.
  • The refrigeration circuit is similar to a domestic refrigerator.
Operation · instrument air dryer — V-502A/B · PSA

How the instrument air dryer works

Twin-tower PSA — adsorption / regeneration
Tower cycle — depressurize · regenerate · repressurize · stand-by
  • The instrument air dryer is a pressure-swing adsorption dryer.
  • Wet compressed air enters the bottom of the «online» tower and flows up through a bed of desiccant (activated alumina); the desiccant adsorbs the water vapour, drying the air to −40 °C pressure dew point in this installation.
  • A small portion of dry outlet air — ~20% of flow — is diverted down through the offline tower. Depressurized, the media desorbs its moisture; the purge air carries it away and vents to atmosphere through a muffler.
Operation · nitrogen generator — V-601A/B · PSA

How the N2 generator works

N2 generator flow — absorber towers, dust filter, buffer tank
  • The N2 generator also works on the PSA principle.
  • Clean dry compressed air enters the bottom of the absorbing tower and flows up through a bed of CMS — Carbon Molecular Sieve — which absorbs oxygen: the O₂ molecule passes into the sieve openings, the N₂ molecule is too large, so it cannot pass in. After a short time the CMS is saturated and must be regenerated.
  • During regeneration the column is depressurized, forcing the absorbed gas out of the CMS; a small portion of product nitrogen flushes the oxygen-rich waste gas to atmosphere through a silencer.
Operation · nitrogen generator — purity control

How the N2 generator works — continued

N2 path — PSA → dust filter → buffer tank → delivery
  • The N2 passes through a dust filter then into the N2 buffer tank.
  • On start-up the buffer holds atmospheric air (78% N2); high-purity PSA nitrogen mixes in, gradually raising purity while low-purity gas continuously vents. At the correct purity the automatic valves switch over and deliver N2 to the system.
  • Consuming more N2 than rated drops the purity — delivery stops automatically, the buffer vents until purity recovers, then delivery resumes.
  • After a stop, the generator takes time to deliver on-spec N2 again.
Operation · end to end

Description of operation — the full path

  • Compressors: 2 × duty + 1 × standby → 10 m³ wet air receiver (V-501).
  • Filtration: 1 µm then 0.01 µm particulate filters → refrigerated dryer — pressure dew point 3–5 °C → activated-carbon filter — residual oil 0.003 mg/m³ with new elements → 2 m³ dry air receiver (V-503).
  • Take-off 1 — instrument air: desiccant dryer + dust filter → instrument air at 7 barg, −40 °C pressure dew point, 1 µm filtration.
  • Take-off 2 — nitrogen: N2 generator PSA (O₂-rich gas to vent) → 1 µm dust filter → 1 m³ buffer tank — auto-vents until the required purity is reached → on-spec N2 to the 10 m³ N2 receiver.
The buffer tank purity cycle — venting until on-spec, then delivering — is automatic; no operator action once started.

Starting sequence — valves

  • Compressor isolation valves open — 500-HV-001 / 002 / 003.
  • Wet air receiver by-pass closed — 500-HV-005.
  • Wet air receiver inlet open — 500-HV-006.
  • Wet air receiver outlet closed — 500-HV-004.

  • Refrigerated dryer outlet filters: one set open, the other closed — (500-HV-017 & 018) or (500-HV-021 & 022).
  • Dry air receiver by-pass closed — 500-HV-033.
  • Dry air receiver inlet open — 500-HV-023.
  • Dry air receiver outlet open — 500-HV-024.

  • Instrument air dryer outlet closed — 500-HV-028.

Start-up · cold start — equipment 1/3

Starting sequence — dryer first, then compressors

  • Turn on the refrigerated dryer («On» button). It must run at least 5 minutes before any compressed air passes through, so it reaches operating temperature.
  • Start 2 of the 3 compressors with the green I button (the E-stop may need twisting to reset). To stop, use the red O button — not the E-stop. The machines load, pressurize the wet receiver, unload, and may drop to standby — they restart automatically when receiver pressure falls.
  • With the wet receiver fully pressurized and the dryer past its 5 minutes, slowly open 500-HV-004 to introduce air into the filters, the refrigerated dryer and the dry air receiver (V-503).

Start-up · cold start — equipment 2/3

Starting sequence — instrument air dryer

  • Once full working pressure is reached in the dry air receiver (V-503), fully open 500-HV-004 and leave it open.
  • Power on the instrument air dryer.
  • Slowly open the inlet valve 500-HV-025 to pressurize the dryer — confirm full working pressure on the front gauge panel.
  • Turn the dryer on with the green On button below the HMI display.
  • Let it run 3 full cycles (≈10 minutes), then partially open the outlet 500-HV-028 so the downstream system pressurizes without overloading the dryer — fully open once the system is pressurized.

Start-up · cold start — equipment 3/3 · N2 package

Starting sequence — N2 package

  • Slowly open the N2 package isolation valve V201/G11/2 to begin pressurizing the system.
  • Turn on the O₂ analyzer on the N2 package control panel — the top panel in the photo.
  • While pressure is building, turn the package on with the green start button on the control panel.
  • Switch the O₂ analyzer on at its on/off switch.
  • The generator may need multiple cycles to reach the required purity in the buffer tank — site ambient conditions and idle time both stretch it.

Maintenance · balance of package — PK-502 refrigerated dryer

Maintenance — refrigerated dryer & filters

  • Every 1 000 operating hours: replace the activated-carbon filters, 2 × 100% (RSG-ACS-0330G/V3)*; check the auto-drain operation; clean the condenser radiator.
  • Every 2 000 operating hours: replace the particulate & coalescing filters, 2 × 100% of each (RSG-AO-0330G/V2 + RSG-AA-0330G/V2); clean the filter auto drains.
* If not replaced at regular intervals, oil mist will contaminate the N2 generator CMS — causing expensive premature failure of the CMS.
