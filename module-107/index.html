What Module 107 covers

Three utilities, one module: compressed air (Unit 500), nitrogen (Unit 600) and raw water (Unit 540). None of them is the product — all three are what keep every other unit in this programme running.

  • Start with the air path end to end: three compressors, a refrigerated dryer, wet and dry receivers, and where the air goes — 23 destinations across the plant.
  • Then nitrogen: how a PSA package separates it from air, the beds the register forgot, the receiver and its relief, and where it goes — 13 destinations.
  • Cross into ESD and interfaces — the panel, PAGA, fail-safe behaviour, the ramp-up sequence and manual valve isolation for both units.
  • Finish on raw water, Unit 540 — the one block built without a digitized P&ID: a truck-fed tank, the reverse-osmosis package, and the line to the amine loop.
  • Two breaks, on the seams between machine blocks, then fifteen minutes of questions and a thirty-minute exam.

From PLC bit to valve stroke

Every solenoid-driven valve on this plant — on-off or control — follows the same chain. It is conceptual: the generic shape every instance takes, not one instrument's datasheet.

PLC
digital output · one bit, on/off
Solenoid
XEV · SDEV · opens/vents the air port
Instrument air
from Unit 500 · the muscle of the chain
Actuator
piston or vane · air pressure into motion
Valve stroke
the valve itself opens or closes
“Electric” is a misnomer. The electrical signal only grants permission — it is instrument air that does the physical work of moving the valve. No actuator working pressure or stroke time is printed: no figure for either is loaded for this module, so the chain stays conceptual rather than instrument-specific.

Block 3 · house theory, not a plant document — principle only — no P&ID, no datasheet for the PSA internals

How PSA separates nitrogen from air

Pressure swing adsorption: under pressure, the bed material holds onto oxygen more than nitrogen, so what passes through is nitrogen-rich. Two beds alternate — one produces while the other regenerates, depressurized.

  • Bed under pressure — air enters, the adsorbent retains O₂, N₂ passes through and leaves the bed
  • Bed depressurized — the same bed vents the O₂ it just captured, regenerating for its next turn under pressure
  • The two beds never run the same phase together — while one produces, the other regenerates, so the outlet never stops
  • Two beds, not one: V-601A and V-601B, each 2.0 kW at 400 V (electrical register) — the alternation is why there are two loads
  • Documented purity range: 93~99.999% — the generator manual's stated operating range, not a setpoint confirmed for this plant
  • No cycle time, no adsorbent type, no working pressure printed here — not sourced for this package
What this slide is not: a P&ID or a datasheet reading. PR39 itself sends the reader to a vendor unit drawing this deck does not hold, so the mechanism is taught as a principle, exactly as the boil-off theory slide of the LNG module was. Purity: m108_r1_kpn39.json, the KPN39 generator manual — range only, no setpoint claimed for this plant.

Block 3 · SAFETY, generic principle — confined-space asphyxiation — not toxic, not flammable

Nitrogen displaces oxygen

Nitrogen is not toxic and not flammable.It is dangerous for one reason: it takes the place of oxygen.
  • Fills a confined or poorly ventilated space
  • Displaces the oxygen that was there
  • No smell, no irritation, no warning — the atmosphere simply stops supporting breathing
  • Same property, used on purpose inside a vessel: blanketing
  • The point of blanketing IS that it displaces oxygen — that is what inertizes the space
  • Outside a vessel, in a space a person can enter, the same displacement is the hazard
No numeric oxygen threshold is printed on this slide — a plant-specific confined-space entry criterion was not confirmed against a safety-philosophy source in this module's research. Treat any specific percentage as an instructor reference, given verbally, not as content this deck verified.

control — instrument air · fail-safe doctrine for pneumatic valves

Fail-safe — how pneumatic valves respond when air is gone

Instrument air does not just run the process — it holds pneumatic valves open or shut. Lose the air, and each affected actuator has to know, on its own, which way is safe.

FCfails closed — spring or air pressure drives it shut on loss of signalFOfails open — spring or air pressure drives it open on loss of signalFLfails locked — holds its last position, moves neither way
  • The selection is made once, at design. An automatic valve is specified with a spring or a fail-safe actuator so that, with no power and no instrument air, it moves to whichever position keeps the process safe — not whichever position it happened to be in.
  • 'Safe' depends on the service, not on a rule of thumb. A valve that feeds fuel or hazardous product typically fails closed; a valve that vents or relieves pressure typically fails open. Which one applies to a given valve is a design decision recorded against that valve, not something read off its tag prefix.
  • This is why an instrument-air failure is treated as a plant event, not a local nuisance. Air-actuated FC and FO valves supplied by the affected header move when its pressure drops far enough — which is exactly the condition PALL-5001 watches on the header itself.
No specific valve's fail position is printed on this face. The register that would carry it (plant_valves) has no fail-position field for any tag in this corpus, and no manufacturer document read for this module states one for a 500/600-area valve. The concept above is standard automatic-valve practice, taught qualitatively until a plant-specific fail-position record is available. No percentage breakdown of FC vs FO valves is claimed — there is no count to give one from.

maint — instrument air · quarterly · the proof, not just the pressure

The quarterly dew-point proof

A pressure gauge reading correctly proves nothing about how dry the air actually is. The dew-point test is the only measurement that does.

why pressure alone does not prove dry air
What pressure confirmsthe compressors and receivers are holding header pressure
What pressure cannot confirmwhether PK-502 is still removing moisture correctly
What can confirm ita dew-point-under-pressure measurement, taken quarterly
  • The test measures dew point at line pressure, not at atmospheric pressure — instrument air is compressed, and moisture behaves differently under pressure than it does once vented. A quarterly reading is compared against the previous ones to catch the dryer drifting before it fails outright.
  • Why it matters downstream: each pneumatic valve fed by this air has an actuator that depends on that air staying dry. Moisture carried into a field actuator can freeze in cold conditions, and a frozen actuator does not move on command — on a valve that may be the one expected to fail to its safe position (slide 38).
  • This is the same PK-502 taught on slide 17. The refrigerated dryer's job is exactly the moisture removal this test is checking. Do not confuse the two: slide 17 is how the dryer works, this test is how the plant proves, quarterly, that it is still working.
No pass/fail dew-point value in degrees is printed here. The training point that sources this test does not carry a numeric threshold, and there is no dryer OEM manual in the register to derive one from — the vendor package covers the compressor train, not the dryer itself. Teach the test and its purpose; do not quote a threshold that is not on record. Source: plant_maintenance_training_points, module 107, priority 20.

Block 5 · theory, no plant numbers — principle of operation · V17 · SAITA quotation, structure only

Reverse osmosis in one slide

Pressure against a semipermeable membrane: water passes, dissolved salts mostly do not. What passes through is the permeate — the product. What is left behind, concentrated, is the concentrate, or reject.

  • Raw water, pressurized, arrives at the membrane
  • Water crosses the membrane → permeate (product) — continues toward V-335
  • Salts stay behind, now concentrated → concentrate (reject) — goes to open drain
  • PK-541A/B is 2×50%: two identical trains doing this in parallel, not one machine with a spare

Module close · eight takeaways — grounded in the slides of this module

Key messages — Module 107

  • Instrument air is the bloodstream of this plant: one line feeds the entire mixed-refrigerant compressor row.
  • In a black start, air is the second thing that starts — before any process unit.
  • Three compressors exist for one duty, and their maintenance is deliberately staggered — not run in step, so the unit is never without a machine mid-service.
  • The quarterly dew-point proof is what separates «there is pressure» from «the air is actually usable» — wet air freezes actuators.
  • A PSA bed separates nitrogen from air, and its filter cartridge is changed only stopped and depressurized.
  • Nitrogen displaces oxygen — the quietest utility in the plant is the one that can kill without warning.
  • When air is lost, each affected pneumatic valve follows its specified fail-safe action — the design intent is to fail toward safe.
  • Unit 540 exists so the amine circuit never runs short of water — and its enemy, in this climate, is freeze.
Every point above traces to a slide earlier in this module — this closing slide introduces no new figure or claim on its own.
