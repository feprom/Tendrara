What Module 104 covers

Unit 350 liquefies the treated gas against a mixed refrigerant compressed by six identical trains, PK-351-1 to PK-351-6, each a two-stage LP + HP oil-flooded screw set on its own P&ID sheet, PR13 to PR18. Five run, one stands by.

  • Walk the MR loop in flow order and say what each stream is doing — compression, precooling, condensing, letting down, boiling back.
  • Name the six trains and their two stages, and say why the plant runs five of six and what the standby is for.
  • Read the lube-oil system as one inventory with two loops and two machines, not as a list of parts.
  • Watch the right numbers. The unit carries 317 instrument tags; know the handful that are protections rather than indications.
  • Take it through start-up and shutdown — the permissives before the sequence, and the overlap at shutdown you must not interrupt.

Why a mixed refrigerant — the cooling curve

Schematic. End points are the C1S design case (F16 +14.77, F19 −149, M1 +0.66, M11/M12 −161.183 °C); the shape between them is drawn for legibility — no composite curve for this plant exists. Composition AT-3581; the nC4 cut may be i-butane, the GC reads both.
  • Gas does not condense at one temperature. The feed is itself a mixture — 89.54 % C1, 8.84 % N₂ (HMB, F16) — so it gives up its heat over a glide: +14.77 down to −149 °C, 164 K, 2 650 kW (DERIVED).
  • A pure fluid does. Ammonia proves it on our own plant: into E-354 at 1.896 °C, out at 1.0 °C — the whole evaporator moves less than 1 K. A flat line under a sloping one is wasted work.
  • A mixture boils over a range. The LP MR leaves V-355B at −161.183 °C and reaches the compressor at +0.66 °C — one fluid across 162 K, absorbing 14 448 kW (DERIVED).
  • So the two curves run parallel. A small approach everywhere: the whole gas duty on one exchanger and one compression loop, instead of a cascade of three fluids and three sets of machines.
why not ammoniaThe ammonia of Module 103 cannot do this job. It boils at −33 °C and freezes at −77.7 °C (NH₃ MSDS) — no pressure takes it to −149 °C. Here it is a pre-cooler only: E-354 takes the HP MR from 55 to 5 °C and condenses 22.5 mol % of it (DERIVED) for 4 318 kW. Everything below 5 °C is the MR.

Cryogenic safety — cold burns, brittle steel, spills and oxygen

Cold metal looks exactly like warm metal, and cryogenic liquid trapped between two closed valves has nowhere to go.On this unit the answer to the second one is thermal relief — TSV-3572 / TSV-3573, set at 5 barg, on the LNG line to V-221
Cold burns
  • Liquid, cold vapour, cold metal
  • Flush with lukewarm water, 38–41 °C
  • Never rub · no hot water, no dry heat
Brittle fracture
  • Carbon steel loses toughness when cold
  • It does not bend — it breaks, no warning
  • Never spill a cryogen on structural steel
Spill behaviour
  • Liquid to gas, about 625 : 1
  • The white cloud is not the hazard edge
  • No water on it — it boils faster
Oxygen
  • CB-356 is N₂-purged · breather 7.5 mbarg
  • T-357 overhead 51.8 % N₂ / 48.2 % CH₄
  • Under 19.5 % O₂ it is SCBA, not a filter

Inside CB-356 — what a brazed-aluminium plate-fin exchanger is

Textbook plate-fin geometry. No internal drawing of either exchanger exists in the document set.
  • Stacked layers, brazed into one block. Corrugated aluminium fins between flat parting sheets, closed at the edges by side bars; the stack is dip-brazed in a vacuum furnace into a single piece of metal. Headers and nozzles are welded on afterwards — no joints inside, no gaskets anywhere.
  • Multistream. One block, several fluids, each with its own layers. E-356B has ten nozzles: six MR and four gas. The MR passes twice (C down, condensing; D up, evaporating) and the gas passes twice (B2, then B3 after the NGL knock-out in V-356B).
  • It is never opened. No removable bundle, no tube to rod, no gasket — and it sits in a perlite-filled box that would have to be emptied first. The only in-situ tools the drawings give you are the core thermocouples TT-3562A…Y for a controlled cool-down (PR11 note ③) and a nitrogen defrost (note ④).
  • Oil carry-over is what kills it. At −157 °C compressor oil is a solid. It plates out in millimetre-high passages and there is no way to get it out. Manual §8.4: a common mechanical oil removal system, S-352, … provides cryogenic-grade oil separation to protect the plate-fin heat exchangers. S-352 is not housekeeping — it is what keeps the cold box alive.

The NRU (1) — why the nitrogen has to come out, and what T-357 is

The three streams are HMB rev 7 case C1S (F20 / F22 / F24) on one common nitrogen scale; the vessel, CV-3551 and X-357 are PR12 rev 7 AS BUILT. The arrangement is ours.
why the nitrogen has to come out
  • Product quality. Manual §8.1 states the duty of the unit in one line: NRU distillation column (T-357) achieving LNG N₂ ≤ 1.1 mol % — the only product-quality figure in the whole of §8.
  • Heating value. Nitrogen is inert. Every mole of it in the LNG is a mole that does not burn — it dilutes the calorific value of the cargo. Boil-off too: nitrogen is more volatile than methane, so it leaves the liquid first and fills the tank vapour space. Principle — no plant figure for it exists in our sources.
  • And it is not vented. Manual Table 8.1: the nitrogen-rich overhead goes to the fuel gas system, through E-372, the NRU vaporizer in Unit 370. It is burned, not lost.
T-357 — a reboiled stripper, not a full column
  • Table 8.3 names it N2 STRIPPER. Random packing, no condenser and no reflux — nothing comes back down the top. 20″ NPS Sch 10S · 7 620 mm TL-TL · SS304 · 1.5 m³ · 10 barg · −180/65 °C · insulation 150 mm.
  • The flash is what separates. The let-down across CV-3551 drops the LNG from 37.85 to 2.50 barg; 7.28 % of it vaporises and the nitrogen goes with that vapour.
  • X-357 does the rest. The reboiler keeps boiling the bottoms so the nitrogen still dissolved in the liquid is driven back up through the packing — which is why the bottoms come out warmer than the feed.
Open topic — the feed nitrogen ceiling. UFD PR06 note ③ reads UNIT 350 IS DESIGN TO WORK UNTIL 7% N2 MAXIMUM; the HMB design case brings 8.84 mol % N₂ into the cold box on F16. Both documents are approved and current — open with ITALFLUID, not settled here.

Block 3 · 3/10 · principle — GEA Grasso LT series · vendor slides 38 / 41 · PR13 sh.1 §2.1, sh.2 §3.1–3.4

How an oil-flooded screw compressor works

  • Two meshing helical rotors in a close-clearance cast-iron casing, 28 barg, −60 / 120 °C. Gas enters between the lobes at the suction end.
  • The pocket seals, then shrinks. It is cut off from suction and squeezed as it travels axially until it uncovers the discharge port. Vi 2.2 is how much it shrinks first.
  • Oil is injected into the compression space — nozzles B (main injection) and B1 (additional injection) on both machines. It seals the rotor clearances, cools the gas and lubricates the bearings.
  • So the discharge is an oil/gas mixture, not gas — 6"-MR-3511xx to V-351-x. The oil separator is the second half of the compressor, not an accessory. That is Block 4.
  • Positive displacement — no surge, no stall. Close the outlet and it keeps pumping until something fails. PR13 sheet 1 carries no PSV: the package has exactly one compressor relief, PSV-3511.H1 @ 28 barg, blocked-outlet case, on the interstage — which is the LP machine's own discharge.
  • Therefore capacity cannot be throttled with a valve. It is changed by the slide valve — next slide.

Take-home · Recap

Key messages — Module 104

  • 1 · Why a MIXED refrigerant. The MR is four components — C1, C2, C4, N₂, watched by chromatograph AT-3581 on the 20″ suction header. A mixture boils over a RANGE of temperature, not at one point, so its cooling curve can follow the gas down. Ammonia alone stops at E-354, near 5 °C; everything colder is MR expanding inside the cold box.
  • 2 · The loop in one sentence: V-358 → PK-351-1…6 (C-351L then C-351H) → S-352 oil removal → E-353-1/2/3 air coolers (99.91 → 55 °C, 1918 kW) → E-354 ammonia cooler (4318 kW, vapour fraction 1.00 → 0.775) → V-355A HP → condensing C pass of E-356B (−157 °C) → PV-3561B expands it into V-355B LP (2.00 barg) → evaporating D pass of E-356B → back to V-358. Closed — it never mixes with the gas, and the MR never enters E-356A, which is the gas-gas exchanger.
  • 3 · Six trains, TWO stages each — C-351L-x + C-351H-x, 5 running + 1 standby. Both motors 560 kW, 690 V, VFD, yet the LP sweeps 5820 m³/h against 1997 m³/h on the HP. HP suction is not only LP discharge — a 6″ feed comes straight off the package suction, so an LP slide-valve move changes HP suction composition, not just flow.
  • 4 · Oil is the machine. One inventory, one separator V-351-x, one cooler E-351-x (10 fans), one pump P-351-x (4 kW) — but TWO separate loops and FOUR filters F-351-xA/B/C/D: A/B on the pumped HP bearing loop, C/D on the unpumped loop that feeds both machines. PDI-3514.Lx/.Hx is CALCULATED as PT-3513.x − PT-3512.x and must stay above 1.5 bar D.
  • 5 · The cold box is not opened. E-356A and E-356B are brazed-aluminium plate-fin cores inside CB-356: no internal drawing, no field repair. What keeps oil out of the MR passes is oil removal S-352 — coalescers V-352-1/2, 15 cartridges each, charcoal V-352-3/4, filters F-352A/B. Oil that gets past S-352 does not come back out.
  • 6 · Capacity control is N−1 plus a swing train. Two modes on one master pair — XC-3501B flow mode (FIC-3560A) and XC-3501A superheat mode — selected on the HMI overview. The rest sit at full load; the swing train modulates its slide valve through PIC-3511.Hx. Below its minimum, XC-3501A opens hot-gas bypass PV-3581 to V-358.
  • 7 · When a train trips. Start HP first, LP second; stop LP first, HP second, with 30 s post-lubrication after every stop — the machine that starts last stops first, so the LP stage never compresses into a stopped HP stage. Twelve machine trips LSD09–LSD20; PSD02 = loss of the MR loop, where Unit 340 had PSD01. Take the standby, do not chase the trip.
