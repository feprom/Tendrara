§1The single line — ELD11 frame, ELD03 content, live values

Two enclosures, four busbars, three ties, nine bays and the whole low-voltage tail: that is ELD11-ING-PR01 rev.2. Every incomer, feeder, cable tag, network analyser and rating inside it is a row of v_sld_nodes / v_sld_edges, digitised from ELD03-ING-PR01…PR04. A busbar is drawn as long as what it carries, so bar D — which feeds the two 690 V panels, both transformers, both inverter-fed drives and the ammonia compressors — is seven columns wide and bar C is two, before a single number has been read. Live P, U, f and PF sit beside the analyser that measures them, which is SICAM's rule and the reason the FMG glyph is on the drop instead of in a caption. Every breaker is DESIGN: the database holds no live open/closed, and none is invented. Under the trend, the machine totals band carries the hour meter and the energy register per set, the load duration curve, and the coverage rulers that answer §5.

§2The conflict the sheet made visible — six of nine bays

ELD11 rev.2 and ELD03 + the INNIO wiring diagram agree on the shape of the plant and disagree on who lives where. Both give 3 · 2 · 2 · 2 machines per bar; both put the same diesel on B and the same diesel on C. And yet six of the nine gas boxes name a different machine. The sheet draws the ELD03 + WD attribution — five independent sources inside the WD carry it (CR-00307), and the live metering, the protection settings and the whole plant model already use it — and puts a red dot on each bay ELD11 places elsewhere. It does not decide; it refuses to hide. Raised as CR-00392 to ITALFLUID.

§3What ELD11 adds that nothing else in the model has

The single-line database stops at 690 V. Everything below it exists only in ELD11, which is why the 400 V panel is drawn as a dashed plate and says so: documented, not modelled.

§4The trend, and what a gap looks like

The one chart the mimic earns. On a plant that hands the load from one machine to the next, the shape is the information: MG2 carried the plant until 06:05, MG1 picked it up at 06:20 and has held ~700–800 kW since — and no instantaneous number above can say that. The collector lost the plant for half an hour around 19:00; that half hour is shaded, not flat, because an hour with no data must never look like an hour at zero.

§5Running hours per generator — and why «last month» does not exist yet

The ask was running hours per generator last month and from the start of the year. A figure for a period is the difference between two counter readings that far apart, and the oldest reading this system holds is from 2026-08-11 21:16. So July is not «zero hours» — it is unread, and writing a number there would be exactly the plausible-and-false kind this project refuses. What is real is drawn instead, all of it: the PAC's own hour meter and energy register, machine by machine — a total since the meter's zero, which no document publishes — with the slice this collector has actually watched in solid ink inside the same bar. On a 254 h register that slice is 2 % of the bar, and that sliver is the answer. Under the rows, three rulers put the number on it: last month 744 h, none of them read; August so far 267 h with 5.7 read; the year 5,355 h with the same 5.7. Every ruler fills itself as the collector keeps running — the shape is already right, only the data is young.

Four sets get a dashed empty row: MG5, MG6, MG7 and MG2D do not exist on any network reachable from OS-001, so GE-005, GE-006, GE-007 and GE-009 have no hour meter at all. Zero hours would be a lie — they are unwatched, not idle. Beside the rows, the load duration curve answers the question the trend cannot: not when the plant was loaded but how much of the time — median 731 kW, 4.8 % of the 15,314 kW installed, which is what a plant in commissioning on one engine looks like.
