1 · The valve family — one body, many actuators

The body is always the bow-tie: two triangles meeting apex-to-apex on the pipe axis. What sits inside it says what it is — hollow circle ball, solid dot globe, nothing gate — and what sits above says how it moves. A rotated square, which is what these diagrams drew before v0.2.0, says only "some valve is here".

2 · The colour rule — the one thing a trainee must not get wrong

Identical shape, different fill. An operating on/off valve and a shutdown valve are the same solenoid symbol; yellow is reserved for the safety family. That is deliberate — the colour is the teaching point. One of these closes on the operator; the other one is normal service. The fill must come from the row's system column, not from the tag prefix — see §7.

3 · The three flags — why 3 194 valves need ~20 bodies and not 60 symbols

G-5: every orthogonal property is a flag. hand puts the hand-wheel on any body, which is how a ball valve tagged HV comes out as a ball that a human turns. ends draws PR00's four end connections. esd is the safety fill. filled is registered and claims nothing: PR00 prints a solid black twin of several valves and nowhere says what it means, so the pack refuses to decide (D-P3).

5 · Plant items and drawing-management marks

The marks in the second half are not equipment — they are statements about the drawing: where scope changes hands, where the piping class changes, where the sheet ends. On a training diagram they matter as much as the valves: they are what tells an operator whose equipment he is looking at.

6 · Ports sit on the pipe axis — the contract that lets a line break

Every in-line symbol exposes ports W and E at y = 0, whatever the actuator does above. That is what lets tam-flow.js procLine() break the pipe at the valve instead of laying the valve on top of an unbroken line. The angle bodies are the exception and say so: a relief valve has ports S (inlet, from below) and E — a PSV is not an in-line device, and drawing it in-line is how people come to think it is one. The 3-way adds S, which is the entire reason it exists.

8 · Off-page connector

The client's own convention, PR00 sheet 1 "P&ID PROCESS CONTINUATION ARROW", read in the field off PRO13-ING-PR50 rev 5. The chevron points the way the fluid goes in both directions — only the anchor moves. A pennant pointing backwards on an inlet is the commonest off-page mistake there is.

9 · An unknown tag draws a visible gap, never nothing

G-4. A duty is not a body: root, bleed, drain and vent say what a valve is for, and PR00 has no body for any of them — a root valve is a globe or a needle depending on the hook-up. They draw the generic body and are counted as gaps in §7 rather than being guessed into a globe.
